# Content Voice & Microcopy Guide

Last updated: March 2026

A reference for anyone writing UI copy, tooltips, error messages, labels, or any text a user reads on the site.
When in doubt, consult this document before writing new copy.

---

## The Voice in One Sentence

A knowledgeable guide who happens to be a wizard — confident, informative, and quietly enchanting.

---

## The Three Rules

### 1. Informative first
The user needs to understand what something does before they appreciate how it's phrased.
Tell them what the thing does. Then, if it fits naturally, give it a little magic.

### 2. Earn the whimsy
Arcane flavor is a reward for good copy, not a replacement for it.
If the magical phrasing makes the meaning less clear, use the plain version.

### 3. Respect their time
DMs are mid-session. Players are waiting for their turn. Every word should earn its place.

## Approved Patterns

### Page Titles
Format: [Tool Name] | Site of Many Things
Example: Initiative Tracker | Site of Many Things

### Button Labels
| Situation | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Primary search action | "Search the Archives" | "Submit", "Go" |
| Add combatant | "Add to the Fight" | "Add" alone,|
| Add to spellbook | "Add to Spellbook", "Write to spellbook" | "Add" |
| Clear filters | "Clear Filters" | "Reset" |
| Copy text | "Copy to Clipboard" | "Copy Text" |
| Support the site | "Buy Us a Mountain Dew" | "Donate", "Support Us" |
| Generic confirm | "Confirm" is fine | "Yes" |

### Empty States
Always include: what happened + what to do next.

| Screen | Copy |
|---|---|
| Initiative tracker, no combatants | "No combatants yet. Add someone to begin the encounter." |
| Spell searcher, no results | "The arcane library found nothing matching those filters. Try adjusting your search." |
| Spellbook, empty | "Your spellbook is empty. Add spells from the searcher to begin." |
| Combat manager, no encounter | "No encounter is active. Start one from the Initiative Tracker." |

### Loading States
Short and calm. Active voice.

| Context | Copy |
|---|---|
| Spells loading | "Searching the archives..." |
| General loading | "Loading..." |
| Saving | "Saving..." |

### Error States
Honest, calm, not alarming. Always suggest a next step if possible.

| Error | Copy |
|---|---|
| Spells failed to load | "Something went wrong loading spells. Try refreshing the page." |
| Generic error | "Something went wrong. Try refreshing the page." |
| No internet | "Could not reach the arcane servers. Check your internet connection and try again." |

### Tooltips
Lead with function. Flavor is optional.
Example: "Filter to spells that require concentration — only one can be active at a time."
Not: "Reveal the mystical spells bound by the caster's concentration..."

### Navigation Labels
Plain and clear. No flavor here — users need to find things fast.
- "Initiative Tracker" not "The Battle Ledger"
- "Spell Searcher" not "The Arcane Index"
- "Articles" or "Blog" not "The Scriptorium" (tempting, but hurts findability)

### Supporter / Ko-fi Copy
Current approved CTA:
"The Initiative Tracker and Spell Searcher are free, and always will be.
If they save you time at the table, consider buying us a Mountain Dew —
it helps keep the lights on and new tools coming."

---

## What to Avoid

- Exclamation points used for excitement (one per page maximum, and only if genuinely earned)
- "Welcome to..." as an opening
- Passive voice in CTAs ("Spells can be filtered by..." → "Filter spells by...")
- Puns that require explanation
- Addressing the user as "adventurer," "traveler," or similar unless it's clearly a one-time easter egg
- All-caps for emphasis (use bold or color instead)
- Ellipses in button labels

---

## Tone by Section

| Section | Tone |
|---|---|
| Tool UI (labels, buttons) | Clean, direct, slight arcane flavor where natural |
| Empty / error states | Calm, helpful, never alarming |
| Loading states | Brief, active |
| Articles / blog | Informative, conversational, DM-to-DM |
| Ko-fi / supporter | Warm, honest, not pleading |
| Newsletter (Buttondown) | Friendly, behind-the-scenes, personal |
| Reddit posts | Genuine, casual, zero marketing language |

## Article Writing Guide

### Voice for Articles
Write like a knowledgeable friend at the table — someone who figured something
out through experience and genuinely wants to pass it on. Not "here is a
feature." More like "here is something I learned that changed how I do this —
let me show you."

The mental model: a wizard sharing a hard-won secret with an apprentice.
Knowledgeable, warm, genuinely glad to help.

### Article Structure
Every article should follow this loose shape:

1. **The problem** — name the friction the reader has felt. Make them nod.
2. **Why it happens** — briefly explain why most approaches fall short.
3. **The insight** — introduce the concept or feature that solves it.
4. **Example(s)** — show it working in a real scenario. Be specific.
5. **The payoff** — what changes for the reader when they use this.
6. **Call to action** — send them to the tool. Light, not pushy.

Examples are the most important section. One strong example beats three
paragraphs of explanation. Two examples are better than one if they cover
different player types or situations.

### Examples: What Good Looks Like
Examples should use a specific character build, level, party composition,
or scenario. Walk through the actual steps concretely. End with a payoff —
ideally with a small moment of humor or recognition if one fits naturally.

Reference: the Fireball example in the Spell Searcher article. The reader
laughs because they recognize themselves. That recognition is what gets
articles shared.

### Language Rules for Articles

**Do not use definitive language.**
Avoid: only, never, always, best, worst, most complete, no one else,
the only tool that, guaranteed.

These feel like marketing. Readers distrust them and they are rarely
literally true.

- Not: "the only spell filter that does this"
- Yes: "most tools don't answer this question — here's one that does"

**Do not punch down at other tools.**
Describe the problem clearly and show how we solve it. Let the reader
draw the conclusion.

**Short paragraphs.** One to three sentences as a target. Readers scan
before they commit. Make it easy to scan.

**Active voice.**
Not "spells can be filtered by class" — "filter spells by class."

### SEO Without Sounding Like SEO
- Include target keywords naturally in the first 150 words
- Use H2 headers a human would actually search for — not keyword stuffing
- Link to the relevant tool within the first 150 words and again near the end
- Meta descriptions should read like a human wrote them — curious and
  specific, not a keyword list

### Pre-Publish Checklist
- [ ] Does the opening paragraph name a real problem the reader has felt?
- [ ] Is there at least one concrete example with specific details?
- [ ] Are all definitive/superlative claims removed?
- [ ] Does the article link to the relevant tool at least twice?
- [ ] Is the meta description written for a human, not a search engine?
- [ ] Would you post this on r/DnD or r/DMAcademy without cringing?
